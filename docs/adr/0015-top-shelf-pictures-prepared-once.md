# The Top Shelf's pictures are prepared once: a card at its exact size, and a tiny blurred glow

The Top Shelf shows what was last watched in the focused app, and the apps' pictures are small: YouTube's Watch Next thumbnails are 320 × 180 or 480 × 360 with black bars (4:3 around a 16:9 frame), others are portrait posters. Stretched across the top of the Home they went soft, so the Top Shelf became a 240 × 135 card beside the words, which left the top of the Home a dark void. The redesign (2026-10-26, `design.pen`, "Home — Top Shelf: luz da obra com capa") lights the top of the Home with the picture's colours and shows the picture as a larger card (256 × 144 dp, 512 × 288 pixels on the TV).

Each picture is prepared once, on a background thread, and the last three are kept:

- **The card** is made at exactly the pixels it covers, so it is drawn one to one and nothing is scaled while drawing. Black bars are cut first. A picture close to 16:9 fills the card; a poster or a square sits whole on a blur of its own colours rather than being cut to a sliver. A picture that had to be enlarged is sharpened (an unsharp mask, stronger the more it was enlarged, up to a limit before it shows), which brings back much of what enlarging softened.
- **The glow** is the picture shrunk to 120 × 40 pixels, blurred, and baked with the dimming the Status Bar and the words need and the fade to black before the App Tray. Drawn stretched across the top, its blur has no detail to lose, which is why a small picture is no problem here; and it costs a frame no more than any picture. A blur effect (`RenderEffect`) would blur the screen again on every frame.

YouTube's thumbnails are also fetched larger where they exist (`maxresdefault`, then `hq720`, both 1280 × 720), for Watch Next as already for the playing video. The glow adds one layer over the top of the Home; the Home's background lost its redundant black layer under its opaque gradient in exchange. On the emulator, with the same moves along the App Tray, the median frame went from 38 ms to about 41 ms (software rendering: a layer weighs far more there than on the TV's GPU) and no stall came from the Top Shelf.
