# Closing an app presses Force stop in Settings

When the owner closes an app in the App Switcher or the System Monitor, the app must really end. `ActivityManager.killBackgroundProcesses`, the only way a regular app can end another, wasn't enough on the owner's TV: it ends only service and cached processes, and apps with services restart by themselves right after (the owner saw "Fechar" do nothing on 2026-10-24). Force stop ends every process and keeps the app from restarting until it is opened again, but Android grants it only to the system (`FORCE_STOP_PACKAGES`), and Setup can't grant that permission.

So the Home presses the button the owner would: the key filter (ADR 0005) already holds accessibility access, and with window content retrieval it opens the app's page in Settings, presses Force stop, confirms with OK and backs out of Settings to where the owner was. It finds the button by Settings' own string (`device_apps_app_management_force_stop`, "Forçar parada" on the TV) and the confirmation by its title, and it presses Back only while Settings is in front. Settings showing for a few seconds bothered the owner (0.3.3), so the filter first takes a still of the screen (`takeScreenshot`, after the Home has taken the closed app off it) and shows it in an accessibility overlay over Settings until Settings is gone; the overlay doesn't take focus, so Settings can still be read and pressed. Remote keys pressed meanwhile are held back, down and up, so none lands in Settings. Two gaps still showed Settings on the TV (0.3.4): the cover was trusted once drawn rather than once its frame was committed to the screen, and Back played Settings' closing animation after the cover came off. Now the filter waits for the commit, and it brings the Home back in front with no animation, as the owner left it, removing the cover only when Settings is off the screen.

Reading a window costs the whole TV something unless it is contained. In Android 12, once a service has looked up a single node, the system keeps an accessibility cache for it, and from then on every app sends every change on screen (content, scrolling, focus) to that service: 199 events from the Home for 10 key presses on the emulator. They arrived on the thread that answers remote keys, and the Home's frame rate dropped after the first close (0.3.3). The filter therefore lists Settings as the only package it listens to (`packageNames`), which leaves key filtering, screenshots and the window list untouched; the Home then sends it nothing. It also asks Android to follow the windows on screen (`flagRetrieveInteractiveWindows`) only for the length of a close.

Apps Android protects, playing media or with a foreground service, are never closed: the owner decided closing should leave them alone. The Open App Limit still closes quietly with `killBackgroundProcesses`, since an automatic close must never flash Settings. Settings itself is never an Open App: it runs the TV's Bluetooth services, comes right back when ended, and opening it to close an app would otherwise make it the most recently used app.

The Controller's overlay (ADR 0016) has a different return surface: the app beneath it must remain in place. For a close from that presentation, Settings opens in a separate document/task without clearing its existing task, the switcher detaches only after the cover is drawn, and Back removes Settings before the switcher is restored under the cover. Each Back waits for that specific window to lose focus; repeating Back while its closing animation was still present also backed out of the underlying app on the emulator. Original window IDs distinguish an already-open Settings screen from the temporary app-management window. Other close entry points keep the return-to-Home path above.

The Controller's injected keys bypass the accessibility filter, so the Android adapter also drops them while a close is busy, without replaying them. Before opening Settings, Force stop drains any key already in progress. The busy check also runs inside the ADB key monitor so a key waiting behind another cannot slip through after that drain.
